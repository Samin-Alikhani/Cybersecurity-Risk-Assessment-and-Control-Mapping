# Executive Brief — Cybersecurity Risk Assessment

## Project context

This case study summarizes an anonymized team assessment of a real nonprofit organization. The source report was based on questionnaire responses. No technical testing of organizational systems was performed.

## Reported practices and gaps

The source report describes backups stored separately from primary systems, account creation and removal, automatic software updates, vendor data-handling agreements, and informal security awareness. It also reports gaps in authentication requirements, assigned security responsibility, written policies, formal awareness training, incident preparation, and technology inventory.

These conditions are reported findings, not independently verified control states. The original questionnaires and supporting evidence were not reviewed for this portfolio.

## Proposed follow-up

1. Confirm important services, responsible owners, and supported MFA coverage.
2. Define incident contacts, reporting instructions, and response responsibilities.
3. Establish scoped technology and service-provider inventories.
4. Gather evidence of update coverage and backup restoration capability.

Priorities are provisional follow-up sequencing. Likelihood, impact, and residual risk have not been rated.

## Independent synthetic lab

A separate lab restored three synthetic files with matching SHA-256 hashes and detected only a deliberately altered copy. The results apply only to the synthetic lab and do not validate the nonprofit's backups or controls.

## Framework and contribution

The original report used CIS Controls v8 and NIST Cybersecurity Framework references. My contribution was framework mapping, including reported gap mappings and NIST references. The register separates source-report mappings from later safeguard-level interpretations.

## Scope limits

No technical control effectiveness, implementation, full IG1 alignment, financial loss estimate, or risk reduction is established by this assessment.
